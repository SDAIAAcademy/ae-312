The break-even calculation

Commit when every month of the term stays above the break-even hours

break_even_hours = commitment_per_month / on_demand_per_hour
# 8,400 / 20 = 420 hours, 56.5% of the month
  • A: lowest month 470. Commit: six months cost SAR 50,400, not 58,580.
  • B: average 347. On demand: two busy months do not pay for four quiet ones.
  • C: falling to 350. On demand: SAR 8,400 a month for SAR 7,000 of hours.
Stable means above break-even for the whole term. Commit the hours you are sure of; buy the peaks on demand.

A 744-hour month: the commitment's discount is 43.5%, so it needs 56.5% of the hours. C looked right on its six-month average and is wrong for the next twelve. The calculation is the same for any committed price: reserved accelerator hours, provisioned throughput or a committed monthly spend with an API provider. Read the provider's terms for the term, what happens to unused hours and whether the commitment can be resized; they differ, and this course does not quote any. With several nodes, commit the number that runs all term and lease the rest by the hour. The stretch of Lab 3.1 asks the same question of Mawj's own node.

Batch tiers

Work that can wait costs half

  • You submit a file of requests; the provider answers them when it has spare capacity.
  • The published terms of the large providers: half the price, results within 24 hours.
  • No streaming and no conversation: each request stands alone and is matched to its answer by an identifier you set.

At Mawj

Khulasa summarises a call after it has ended. Nobody is waiting on the line. It has run on the interactive tier since its first day.

The lab models the batch tier from data/hosting/batch_tier.json: a discount of 50% and a recorded distribution of completion times. Real terms differ by provider (size limits per batch, expiry of results, which models are offered): read the page of yours. Ask the room for a workload of theirs that nobody waits for: nightly classification, document extraction, evaluation runs. Evaluation runs are the easiest win of all. Source: Claude Platform documentation, batch processing.

Before it ships

A batch job needs the same controls as an interactive one

Attribution

The batch carries the tags of its application, or its spend lands in "unallocated" on the showback.

Budget

One submitted file can hold a day's spend. The budget is checked before submission, not per request.

Some requests of a batch fail or expire. The job resubmits those, and counts them.
